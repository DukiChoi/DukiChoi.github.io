An ADC samples a continuously varying voltage at regular intervals. If the sampling rate is too slow, those samples can resemble a completely different, slower signal. This is aliasing.

Nyquist frequency

Half the sampling frequency is the Nyquist frequency. Higher-frequency components can fold into lower frequencies. The apparent frequency is the absolute difference between the signal frequency and its nearest integer multiple of the sampling frequency. For example, sampling 45 Hz at 50 Hz makes it appear as |45 − 50| = 5 Hz.

To reconstruct the signal: sampling frequency > 2 × highest signal frequency

In practice

Once sampled, an aliased component cannot be distinguished from a true low-frequency signal using those samples alone. An analog low-pass anti-aliasing filter removes unwanted components before the ADC. Real filters need a transition band, so choose a sampling rate with enough margin; 5–10 times the frequency of interest is a useful illustrative choice, not a universal requirement.
