Both I2C and SPI use a controller-generated clock to time serial data. I2C addresses multiple devices over two wires. A typical four-wire SPI link selects a peripheral and can send and receive simultaneously.

I2C: two wires, address-based selection

I2C uses SCL for clock and SDA for data. Pull-up resistors hold the lines high; devices drive them low using open-drain outputs. This allows devices to share the lines without actively driving opposite logic levels.

START is SDA falling while SCL is high, followed by a 7-bit address and read/write bit. The addressed target acknowledges by pulling SDA low. Data changes while SCL is low and is stable while SCL is high; SDA transitions during SCL high indicate START or STOP.

SPI: four wires and simultaneous exchange

SPI uses SCK, controller output MOSI, peripheral output MISO, and chip select CS. Selecting a peripheral with CS avoids an address field, and SPI has no standard ACK bit. Over eight clocks, each side shifts out and receives one byte.

Mode 0 keeps the clock low when idle and samples on rising edges. Modes 1–3 use other clock polarity (CPOL) and phase (CPHA) combinations. Always match the peripheral's datasheet settings.
